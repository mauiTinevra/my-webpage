Six small businesses that don't exist, and the sites they deserve.

A record shop, a pottery studio, a barber, a florist, a stargazing guide and a bookshop, all imagined around Phoenix. Each site puts the customer's errand first and has one moment that really works: music made in the browser, a pot you shape, a chair you book.

Read how they were made
  • 01

    Ocotillo Records

    A used vinyl shop on Roosevelt Row, printed like a two-color poster.

    Pick a record and press play. The music is made live in your browser, with no audio files.

    Visit the siteIts guide
  • 02

    Monsoon Clay

    A pottery studio in Tempe, set like a quiet studio catalog.

    Drag four marks to throw a pot. The page names what you made and the class that teaches it.

    Visit the siteIts guide
  • 03

    Camelback Barber

    A four-chair barbershop with an enamel sign and a tiled floor.

    A chair board that follows the real clock in Phoenix, and a booking that ends in a ticket stub.

    Visit the siteIts guide
  • 04

    Paloma Flores

    A florist in Melrose, made of cut paper.

    Build a bouquet stem by stem. It arranges itself in the vase, and the builder is the order form.

    Visit the siteIts guide
  • 05

    Dark Sky Sonora

    Stargazing tours north of Scottsdale, kept like an observatory logbook.

    Tonight's real sky, computed for Scottsdale: the moon, the planets and when it gets dark.

    Visit the siteIts guide
  • 06

    Dos Gatos Books

    A used bookshop in Mesa with two cats, laid out like a literary quarterly.

    A shelf of forty invented books. Pull one, read the staff note, and put it on your hold slip.

    Visit the siteIts guide

Every business here is imaginary. The sites are concepts directed by Maui Tinevra and built with AI agents working from written briefs. The guide explains exactly how.
