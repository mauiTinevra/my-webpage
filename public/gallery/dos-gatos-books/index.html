    From the counter

    Every book in here has already been read once

    By Rosa Albarrán, who owns the place

    In the margin The shop opened in 2012 in what used to be a shoe repair. The counter is the cobbler’s bench.

    People ask why we only sell used books, and the true answer is that I like knowing a book has survived somebody. A new book is a promise. A used one is a reference: someone carried it on a bus, argued with it in pencil, pressed a bus transfer between its pages and then let it go.

    We have about nine thousand of them in a room the size of a two-car garage. Fiction runs along the east wall. Poetry has its own shelf, bay three, at eye level, because poetry kept on the bottom shelf is poetry nobody bends down for.

    A used book has been loved once already. We only handle the introductions.

    In the margin June mends what she can. Ask to see the drawer of things found in books: 212 bookmarks, one pressed scorpion.

    The cats came with the lease, more or less. Tinta walked in during our first monsoon and has supervised the shelving ever since. She will follow you to whatever you take down, to check your judgment. Punto arrived two years later in a box of cookbooks and has been asleep, give or take, from that day to this.

    Come on a Friday. We push the dollar cart aside, put out thirty folding chairs, and somebody reads. It is free, the coffee is bad, and the open mic is five minutes each, which I enforce with a kitchen timer shaped like a tomato.

    Before you come

    Finding us, and selling us your books

    Find us

    118 West Main Street, Mesa, Arizona 85201, on the north side, under the green awning with two cats painted on it. The light rail stops two blocks east. There is free parking behind the building off the alley.

    Sell us your books

    Tuesdays, 10 am to 2 pm, up to two boxes at a time. We pay cash on the spot, or a third more in store credit. We cannot take textbooks, encyclopedias or anything that has been wet.

    The poetry shelf

    Bay three, eye level, about four hundred books, restocked every Wednesday. Chapbooks by Arizona poets sit face out on the top row. Local poets can leave five copies on consignment.

    Write to us

    Looking for something particular? We keep a want list in a shoebox and we do check it.
